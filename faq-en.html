How is knitted soft skin different from a rigid shell?

It is lighter, breathable, removable and designed around joints, sensors and ports.

Which robots are suitable?

Humanoid, service, rehabilitation, exoskeleton, dexterous-hand and research platforms.

What project data should we provide?

Images, dimensions, 3D models, joint range and interface locations.

Can the skin be washed?

Yes; electronics require dedicated protection and removable interfaces.

Will it affect robot cooling?

Mesh zones and openings are designed around thermal areas.

How can a knitted robot neck support motion, recovery and cooling?

The neck is compact but must handle multi-axis motion and repeated bending. We use one-piece shaped knitting with stretch distributed along joint motion, so the cover follows rotation and naturally recovers, helping reduce bunching, slack and bulging. Knit porosity supports heat release from internal motors and wiring, while assembly openings, service access and removable structures can be reserved. We can deliver the full path from structural-data review and textile development to production covering, or develop the neck as a standalone custom part.

Can cameras and ports remain open?

Yes. Sensors, vents, charging and service access are included in engineering.

Can sensors be integrated?

Pressure, touch and connection interfaces can be evaluated per project.

Can we order a prototype or small batch?

Yes. Knitting supports R&D samples and flexible small-batch validation.

What is the typical lead time?

A basic first prototype usually takes around 15 days after data approval.

What can dexterous-hand data capture record?

Finger motion, posture, pressure or contact data, depending on the hand structure.
